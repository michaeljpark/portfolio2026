Overview

One board for money that actually moves

Most budgeting apps ask you to log in, dig through menus, and reconcile numbers that are already a day old. finboard flips that — your accounts across Scotiabank, TD, BMO, and RBC land on a single live board, and the number you check most often sits right on the lock screen: today's spend, and how much is left.

Designed for students and first earners, finboard turns a stream of raw transactions — Campus Coffee, Transit Pass, Dining Hall, Ramen Spot — into something legible at a glance, so a quick check replaces the monthly reckoning.

Problem

Budgeting fails at the moment of spending

The decision that breaks a budget — buy the coffee, tap the transit pass, order the ramen — happens in seconds, phone in hand. But the tools meant to help live a login and three taps away, and the balance they show is stale. By the time you reconcile, the month is already gone.

For students juggling several bank accounts, the problem multiplies: money is scattered, statements arrive late, and there is no single place that answers the only question that matters in the moment — can I afford this right now?

Design Principles

Glanceable first. Effortless always.

finboard is built so the most important number reaches you before you open anything. The lock-screen widget shows today's spend and what's left; open the app and the same board expands into a live transaction feed and budget rings.

Nothing to log, nothing to categorize by hand. Accounts sync, transactions self-sort, and the interface stays quiet until a number needs your attention.

Core Features
01
Lock-screen widget
Today's spend and remaining balance live behind a private "Tap to view" glance — the check you make twenty times a day, without unlocking or opening the app.
02
Unified bank feed
Scotiabank, TD, BMO, and RBC accounts flow into one chronological feed. Each transaction carries its bank, last-four, merchant, time, and amount — no switching apps.
03
Budget rings
A daily allowance renders as a single progress bar — spent versus left — so pacing is felt, not calculated. Categories auto-sort from the merchant.

Prompt to product in one pass — finboard was designed, built, and shipped through AutoQ.

finboard started as a one-line brief — "make a live dashboard to track personal transactions that fully works" — and became a case study in how far a single prompt can go when the design intent is clear. The lock-screen-first framing, the unified bank feed, and the student-scale budget all came out of asking what a person actually needs in the two seconds before they spend.
