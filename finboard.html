Overview

A widget-first banking dashboard for financial cognition.

As payments go cashless, the felt sense of spending money fades, and budgeting apps that are good at collecting data rarely turn it into self-understanding. finboard reframes spending as something to explore rather than track.

I treated native widgets as new interaction points and designed them to optimise financial cognition, so a person can understand their money without opening an app or logging in.

Problem definition

Your receipt is not visible.

Canadian bank apps already ship personal-finance tools, but they sit deep inside the app and go unused, and no major bank surfaces spending across institutions. I framed the problem around access rather than features: how do you put a person's own financial picture in front of them, at the moment and place they would actually glance at it?

Discovery

Information beats features.

I opened the research by fixing the target and the bet: an iOS-native widget dashboard for students and early-career adults in the Canadian banking context, on the hypothesis that surfacing bank data as a widget, outside the login, would improve how people see their own spending.

Then I mapped the pain across three fronts at once. The weak link is not the tools themselves but their adoption, and the users who struggle most gain the most from low-effort, always-visible cues.

01

Three fronts

The market, in-app behaviour, and what crosses banks

The market

  • PFM is the weak link. Basic transactional tools score well; spending analysis and budgeting fail to connect with many users.
  • Adoption, not availability, is the bottleneck: promoting wider tool use is the first hurdle.
  • Canada lagged the US on PFM "super tools", a structural gap, not an incidental one.
J.D. Power Canada, 2023

In-app behaviour

  • Information ranks first of four satisfaction factors in 2026, ahead of tools, performance and design.
  • Category satisfaction is flat at 678 of 1,000. Apps feel interchangeable.
  • Alerts already move the needle: +33 points for financially vulnerable users. Passive signals work.
J.D. Power Canada, 2023 and 2026

Across banks

  • Bank widgets today are balance-only. Scotiabank's quick-balance widget shows no spending at all.
  • No Big Five bank offers a cross-institution spending widget, so multi-bank students see a fragmented picture.
  • Everything of value sits behind the login: unlock, open, authenticate, navigate, interpret.
Scotiabank Help Centre; desk research

04

The rules

Canada's Consumer-Driven Banking Act, read as design input

What the Act saysWhat finboard does with it

The Act received Royal Assent in March 2026. The Bank of Canada supervises it and maintains a public registry.

Setup picks the bank from that registry, then authenticates at the bank itself.

Express consent is valid for no longer than 12 months, with defined renewal triggers, and records of it are kept for five years.

Consent renews every 12 months. A lapsed consent degrades the widget instead of breaking it.

In scope: balances, and completed, pending and pre-authorized transactions, phasing in by account type.

Exactly what Today, Transactions and the weekly pace read. Nothing on a widget needs more.

Derived data, information significantly enhanced to add usefulness or commercial value, is excluded from the Act.

finboard's categorisation and insight layer is defensible IP rather than shared data.

About nine million Canadians share banking credentials through screen scraping. Its prohibition is written in but not yet in force.

finboard connects through consent, read-only, and never stores an online banking password.

Prototyping

Settle the structure, then make it feel native.

I prototyped in two passes. The first projected the app onto surfaces iOS already provides, built out of native components rather than an invented shell, so the structure could be argued about before any visual style existed. The second made it feel like a real iOS product.

Design audit

Contrast measured, not judged by eye.

I took the finished screens back into Figma and measured every layer against WCAG 2.2 AA. It turns "is this grey readable" into a number I either meet or I don't, and it is the standard the work would be held to in practice anyway.

In Ontario that is a legal floor rather than a preference. The AODA sets WCAG AA as the bar for public-facing digital services, and I held the screens to 2.2, the current version of it.

Reflection

Human-centred design, better access to the data.

For now, how far this can go is limited. Security, iOS’s rules for widgets and Live Activities, and each bank’s own policies all restrict which spending data can leave the bank app and where it can appear.

Even so, the research showed that access to that data can be improved: the reading moves onto the surfaces people already look at, and an added check, Face ID, keeps it private until it is you looking. The roadmap goes two ways from here.
