How OnGen Radio works

OnGen plays short spoken shows between your plans, written from your calendar, mail, reminders and AI chats. The phone on this page runs the actual prototype. Each section below shows one of its cards, and you can use the card directly.

Today's shows on a timetable

The top screen lays out John's day on a vertical time scale. Each show sits at its air time, and the red line marks the current time. The timetable stays still until you scroll it.

  • Brightness shows where a show sits in the day: white on air, mid grey for the next show, dim for the previous one and darker for the rest.
  • Drag up or down to look through the day. When you let go, it returns to the current time after 2.5 seconds.
  • Tap a show to load its program into the card below. Music blocks are listed but can't be edited.

Editing a show's program

Each show is made of corners, short segments such as Calendar or Local. One bar shows how the show's minutes are split between them, and longer corners are drawn brighter.

  • Drag a divider to move minutes between two corners. Each corner keeps at least 2 minutes, and the show's total length stays the same.
  • Labels move with their blocks and shift aside so they don't overlap.
  • Edit shows a remove button on each corner and chips for corners you haven't added, up to six in total. A new corner takes up to 5 minutes from the longest one.

Choosing sources and generating a show

The connection card lists where John's information comes from: App Connector for mail and AI chats, and Smart Speaker for listening at home. The card below it shows the next show and the Generate key. The key is the only element on the home screen that glows, since it is the only way to put a show on air.

  • Apps that are off show in grey at 28% opacity, and apps that are on show in full colour. The status reads Connected while at least one app is on.
  • Each toggle updates the plan. If no mail app is connected, the host skips the email step.
  • Generate runs five steps, 0.65 s apart: calendar, mail, AI chats, writing the corners and voicing the host. The logo turns into a spinner and the caption counts the steps.
  • A finished show is marked out of date when a source changes, and the screen reads Program changed, press Generate.

The player shows the script line by line

Opening a show expands the screen from 300 to 554 px and hides the cards below. The script replaces the timetable.

  • The current line is full size and white. Other lines shrink to 84% and fade, and lines more than two away drop to 40% opacity.
  • Words light up as they're read. With sound on, the highlight follows the voice. On this page the voice is muted, so the highlight moves every 0.32 s.
  • The waveform has one bar per word. Spoken words are tall, pauses are flat, and the red needle stays at 58% of the width.
  • The corner name, its length and the elapsed time sit at the bottom, with the time in a 64 px light numeral.

The Now Playing panel

If you go back to the home screen while a show is playing, a red panel opens from the camera cutout. It shows the current line, a waveform and the time left. Pausing folds it back into the island, and tapping the island opens it again.

  • It opens from the island. The panel starts at the island's 124 × 36 size and expands to full width, while the key card and mode dial fold away.
  • The program bar follows playback. The corner on air is brightest, the next one is at half brightness and the rest are dim.

The mode dial

The bottom row sets how often OnGen airs: Daily, Hourly or Weekly Radio. You choose by moving a ruler under a fixed red needle.

  • Labels are 120 px apart above a tick every 8 px, and get brighter as they approach the needle.
  • Drag the ruler, tap either side or use the arrow keys. When you let go, it settles on the nearest mode.
  • In this prototype the dial only changes the display. The timetable above shows the Hourly schedule.

Colour, type and shape

The interface follows the look of a radio, with flat dark keys around a black screen. Four greys build the structure, and red is used only for something that is on air or about to air.

Your day as a short radio show

OnGen is an AI-curated listening app. It reads your calendar, mail, AI chats and location, and plays short shows between your plans about what you need to do, what you missed, what to remember and what's happening nearby. This page covers the research and the decisions behind it.

Most AI waits to be asked

Radio reaches people without being asked. AI assistants know a lot about your inbox, calendar and city, but they only answer questions. OnGen plays like radio and uses what AI knows about your day.

What a show covers

Each show is made of short corners, and each corner covers one of four things: your work for the day, what changed while you were away, what you might forget, and what's happening nearby.

Shows air in the gaps of your calendar

Shows are placed between meetings and commutes. Each one fits its gap and uses the corners that suit that time of day, such as a wake-up briefing at 7:30, traffic and errands on the way home, and a recap in the evening. Your own music service plays in between.

Three service layers

Regional data feeds a shared community stream, and the same engine narrows it down to personal shows. The inputs and outputs of each layer were mapped before the build started.

Seven research phases

Each of the four journey steps had a hypothesis, a survey question and a target metric before anything was built. Consent was tested first, since that is where people were most likely to drop off. Each phase below lists what was found and what was decided as a result.

Interested, but not yet comfortable

Every average in the concept survey fell between 2.91 and 3.41 on a 5-point scale, close to neutral. Local news and community events were what people most wanted to hear. The open answers split evenly, with 13 excited about local information in one place and 13 worried about being listened to, so consent became part of the interface. Usability was already above average in Pilot 1.

Built as a working web app

OnGen runs on a phone. A local Python server handles the AI and backend, a prompter turns sources into scripts, AI APIs generate the voice, and Firebase logs every interaction. The GradEX 111 numbers come from those logs.

Findings

Visitors reached the core action

About a third of visitors created their own show, at an exhibition where a lot of other work was competing for attention.

The first 30 seconds matter most

47.7% of users left within 30 seconds, but 23.4% stayed ten minutes or more. The first show needs to start right away, before any setup.

Signed-in visitors stayed longer

Registered users stayed about 20 times longer and made 56 times as many interactions. Personal context is what keeps people listening, so sign-in should come earlier and take less effort.

Trust needs to be designed

The neutral survey scores came from worry about being listened to. Clear consent copy and visible control over sources belong in the product itself.
